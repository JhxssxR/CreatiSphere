ERP System Overview (1–2 Minutes)[Visual: Title Slide / Login Screen]Speaker: "Hello everyone. Welcome to our presentation of CreatiSphere, a comprehensive SaaS ERP system designed for the digital creator economy.CreatiSphere serves as a centralized platform that bridges digital creators and customers while providing robust backend management for administrators.Our target users include Creators who manage their digital assets and commissions, Customers who browse and purchase these assets, and internal staff like Sales and Finance teams who monitor the platform's health.The system encompasses several main modules: Customer Management (CRM), Asset & Catalog Management, Sales & Transactions, Finance & Reporting, and an overarching Admin Dashboard."---B. SaaS Business Model and Client Transactions[Visual: User Registration / SuperAdmin Users Page]Speaker: "CreatiSphere operates on a SaaS business model. Instead of buying software, users access our platform to conduct their business.Let’s walk through the subscription and onboarding process. When a user registers, they can be assigned different roles—such as a Customer or a Creator.(Demonstrate logging in as a Creator or Customer)While customers get access to the marketplace and order tracking, Creators have access to a specialized suite of tools: the Asset Editor, Catalog Manager, and Commission tracking.We can expand this to include tiered SaaS plans (like Basic vs. Premium Creator accounts) where higher tiers allow for more asset uploads or lower commission fees.(Show the login process and how the UI dynamically routes the user to their specific Dashboard based on their role)"---C. Database Design and ERP Relationship Explanation[Visual: Display ERD diagram on screen or discuss the DatabaseService structure]Speaker: "Our system relies on a well-structured relational database using SQL Server.The core tables include `Users`, `Products` (Digital Assets), `Transactions` (Orders), and `Reports`.The primary relationships are designed to support our business rules:A `User` (Creator) has a one-to-many relationship with `Products`.A `User` (Customer) has a one-to-many relationship with `Transactions`.Each `Transaction` acts as a junction, linking a Customer, a Creator, and a Product.This normalized structure ensures that when a Customer purchases an asset, inventory metrics are updated, the Creator's sales dashboard reflects the sale, and Finance reports aggregate the transaction accurately."---F. ERP Workflow and Data Flow Logic[Visual: Follow a single data flow - e.g., creating a product to selling it]Speaker: "Let’s look at the data flow logic.Input: A Creator inputs a new digital asset in the `CatalogManagerPage`.Processing & Storage: The system validates the input and stores the asset in the database via our `DatabaseService`.Flow to Customer: The asset immediately becomes visible in the Customer’s `BrowseMarketplacePage`.Transaction: The customer places an order. The system processes the payment logic and records the transaction.Output Generation: The transaction triggers updates across the system—the Sales Dashboard updates its revenue progress bar, and the Finance module generates updated business reports."---H. Validation and Error Handling[Visual: Form submission with empty fields or invalid data]Speaker: "To maintain data integrity, CreatiSphere includes robust validation.(Demonstrate trying to submit a form without required fields or entering invalid data)As you can see, the system prevents the operation and displays clear error messages to the user.In the backend, we use `try-catch` blocks during database operations. If a connection fails or duplicate data is detected, the system gracefully handles the error without crashing, ensuring a seamless user experience."---J. Technical Explanation and Understanding[Visual: Show a glimpse of Visual Studio / Code structure (MVVM, XAML, C#)]Speaker: "From a technical standpoint, CreatiSphere is built using .NET MAUI (Multi-platform App UI) for a cross-platform desktop experience, utilizing C# and XAML.We followed an architecture that separates our Views from our Data Services (`DatabaseService.cs`).A major challenge we encountered was dynamically binding UI elements—like the interactive summary cards and progress bars in our Dashboards—to real-time database queries. We solved this by leveraging `BindableLayouts` and asynchronous database calls (`Task<T>`), ensuring the UI remains responsive while data loads.Ultimately, building this ERP system demonstrated how critical integrated systems are for real-world businesses, ensuring that data flows seamlessly from creators to customers and all the way to financial reporting."
